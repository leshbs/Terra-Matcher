# Mission rehearsal starts on Earth.

Before a rover navigates Mars, teams need better questions about the terrain it will face. Terra compares a selected Martian landscape with curated places on Earth—turning topographic data into transparent analog hypotheses for exploration, testing, and learning.

**Explore the Mars scenario**  
**See how the method works**

*Data-derived similarity—not mission-site approval.*

## Start with a 20 km window in Gale Crater.

Our first Mars scenario focuses on Aeolis Palus, a defined study window within Gale Crater. Terra reads its terrain through three comparable signals: median slope, terrain roughness, and relative relief.

It then compares their normalized patterns against curated Earth candidates, while preserving the source, resolution, and limits behind every result.

**One Mars reference zone. Five Earth candidates. Three terrain signals.**  
Every comparison is a starting point for investigation—not a final answer.

---

## From a Mars terrain question to an Earth analog hypothesis.

### 1. Select a Mars scenario

Begin with a defined reference window—not a vague image of Mars. Explore the scenario through topography, slope, and terrain-roughness layers.

### 2. Read the terrain profile

Terra translates each area into comparable terrain signals: median slope, terrain roughness, and relative relief. Raw units, source resolution, and coverage stay visible.

### 3. Compare curated Earth candidates

The similarity engine ranks candidate locations by their normalized terrain patterns. A score reflects the selected measurable proxies, not an absolute claim of equivalence.

### 4. Inspect the explanation

Open a side-by-side comparison to see what matches, what differs, how much data supports the result, and which real-world conditions remain unmodeled.

---

## Explore the match. Inspect the evidence. Question the gap.

Terra makes cross-planet terrain comparison understandable without hiding its complexity. Instead of presenting a black-box “best match,” it shows which Earth candidates resemble a Mars scenario on measurable terrain signals, what drives the ranking, and where the analogy breaks down.

### See beyond visual resemblance

Compare slope, roughness, and relative relief—not just landscapes that look Martian.

### Trace every conclusion

Each result is tied to a model run, feature contribution, data coverage, and dataset provenance.

### Turn curiosity into testable hypotheses

Use ranked candidates to frame questions for rover mobility, terrain navigation, instrument testing, or geological-process study.

---

## Better preparation begins with clearer evidence.

Terra helps make planetary analog research more legible and reproducible. It gives students, science communicators, and future exploration teams a shared way to move from a Mars terrain question to an Earth-based candidate hypothesis—while keeping uncertainty visible.

By making both similarity and limitation first-class, Terra encourages more responsible exploration conversations: better comparisons, clearer assumptions, and stronger next questions before field validation begins.

**Open Earth and Mars data, transformed into an explainable tool for identifying, characterizing, comparing, and communicating terrestrial analog hypotheses.**

---

## The similarity score is not the answer.

A high score begins an investigation. It does not approve a field site, prove geological equivalence, or replace field validation.

Terra does not model gravity, atmospheric pressure or composition, radiation, global temperature regimes, biological activity, access, safety, or land permissions.
